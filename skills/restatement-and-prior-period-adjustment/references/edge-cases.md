## Edge Cases

All eleven preserved, in order.

**Error versus change in estimate**: the threshold question. **New information = estimate change (prospective); something wrong given what was known = error (retrospective). Getting this wrong is itself a misstatement.** `[manual]`. **The amounts are identical either way**, which is precisely why the ledger cannot decide it. Document the basis carefully.

**Little r restatement**: prior-period errors immaterial individually, whose correction would be material to the current period — **restate the immaterial prior periods rather than take a material current-period hit. Commonly misunderstood.** `[auto]` for the test (Step 4); `[manual]` to conclude.

**Qualitative materiality**: an arithmetically small error can be material — turns a loss to a profit, breaches a covenant, affects a metric, involves fraud. **Never rely on a quantitative threshold alone.** `[auto]` for four of the seven factors; `[manual]` for the conclusion.

**Public company consequences**: a Big R restatement typically triggers a **non-reliance determination, amended filings, a likely material weakness conclusion, possible compensation clawback, and regulatory and legal scrutiny. A major event — flag for legal and regulatory advisors and governance.** `[manual]`, entirely, and flagged at the outset because timetables start running.

**Tax effect**: corrections almost always have current and/or deferred tax effects and **may require amended tax returns.** `[auto]` for the accounting effect; `[manual]` for the returns.

**Multiple errors**: **assess aggregated and individually — several immaterial errors can aggregate to material.** `[auto]`, and this is arithmetic a tool should insist on doing, because the failure mode is a series of individually-reasonable dismissals.

**Error in interim periods**: interim restatement rules and their relationship to annual periods need care. `[manual]`. See `quarter-end-close` — and note that an error corrected within the same fiscal year is a different problem from one crossing a year end.

**Fraud-related errors**: coordinate with `fraud-detection-and-forensics`, legal and governance; **heightened scrutiny and obligations.** `[manual]`. And carry across that skill's discipline: **report what the data shows, with figures, never as an allegation.**

**Covenant and contractual impacts**: a restatement can breach covenants or trigger contractual consequences — **assess and flag.** `[auto]` to recompute, given the definitions; `[manual]` for the definitions and the consequences. See `debt-and-covenant-tracking`.

**Comparative periods in future filings**: **once restated, the corrected comparatives carry forward; ensure consistency in subsequent statements.** `[auto]` to check — and this is a real ongoing test: read the next period's comparatives against the restated figures and confirm they agree. **A restatement that is not carried into the following year's comparatives creates a second inconsistency on top of the first.**

**Auditor involvement is mandatory**: restatements of audited statements require auditor involvement and likely re-issued audit opinions. **Not a unilateral management exercise.** `[manual]`, and it deserves the last word among the edge cases for a reason. **A complete-looking workpaper can create the impression the work is finished.** It is not finished; on audited statements it cannot properly have been *started* without the auditor. Sheet 5 says so on its face.

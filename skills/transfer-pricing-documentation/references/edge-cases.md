## Edge Cases

All eleven preserved, in order.

**Unique or valuable intangibles on both sides**: **simple one-sided methods (TNMM) may not work; profit split may be required. DEMPE analysis — development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, exploitation — determines who should earn intangible returns. Highly complex; specialist required.** `[manual]`. **`[auto]` for one input**: **DEMPE activities cost money**, and R&D, marketing and legal spend by entity is readable — which shows where the activities are actually performed, whatever the IP ownership says.

**Intercompany financing**: **loans must bear arm's-length interest — credit rating of the borrower, terms, currency. Thin-capitalisation and interest-limitation rules also apply. Cash pooling has specific guidance.** `[auto]` for balances and interest charged; `[manual]` for the rate benchmarking and the borrower's standalone credit rating. See `tax-return-prep-corporate`, whose interest limitation uses the same inputs. **`[auto]` for one common finding: an intercompany balance bearing no interest at all**, which is a financing transaction priced at zero.

**Business restructurings**: **moving functions, risks or intangibles between entities can trigger exit charges. Significant TP and valuation issues; specialist required.** `[manual]`. **`[auto]` to detect the shape** — a material shift in an entity's cost profile or revenue between years is what a restructuring looks like in the numbers, and it is easy to miss when each year is documented separately.

**Limited-risk versus full-risk characterisation**: **the risk that a tax authority recharacterises the entity is a common dispute. Substance must support the characterisation.** **`[auto]` corroboration — Step 2b**, and it is the edge case that section was written for.

**Year-end adjustments / true-ups**: **groups often true up intercompany pricing to land within the range; ensure these are documented, agreement-supported, and consistent with customs and VAT treatment.** **`[auto]` to detect** — a year-end journal adjusting intercompany pricing has a recognisable shape. **And one observation to report factually: a true-up landing the result exactly at the median, every year.** That is pricing being managed to the range rather than set at arm's length. It may be entirely proper and agreement-supported; **the pattern is worth surfacing, with its figures, and never as an allegation.**

**Customs and VAT interaction**: **TP adjustments can affect customs valuation and VAT. Coordinate so a TP adjustment doesn't create a customs or VAT problem.** `[manual]`. **A downward TP adjustment on goods can mean customs duty was overpaid — and an upward one can mean it was underpaid.** See `vat-gst-international`.

**Loss-making tested party**: **tax authorities scrutinise entities that consistently lose money. A limited-risk entity making losses is a red flag requiring strong support.** **`[auto]` — Step 6b**, and it is the highest-yield indicator here.

**CbCR threshold and filing**: **large groups must file; the threshold and mechanics vary. Confirm current rules and the filing entity and jurisdiction.** `[manual]` for the rules; **`[auto]` for the consolidated revenue** that the threshold turns on.

**Penalty protection**: **contemporaneous documentation prepared by the filing deadline often provides penalty protection. Timing matters — flag deadlines.** `[manual]`. **This is the highest-consequence deadline in the skill**: the same documentation, prepared late, may not protect against penalties at all. **Flag deadlines at the start of the engagement, not in the output.**

**Developing-country and special regimes**: **some jurisdictions have safe harbours, specific methods or unique requirements. Confirm local rules.** `[manual]`.

**Substance over form**: **the analysis must reflect actual conduct, not just contracts. Where they diverge, tax authorities follow conduct.** **`[auto]` for the conduct — Step 8.** **This is the principle the whole skill rests on, and it is the one the ledger speaks to most directly.**

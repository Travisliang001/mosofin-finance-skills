## Edge Cases

All eleven preserved, in order.

**Over-claiming boundaries**: the most common error and audit trigger — claiming entire projects rather than only the uncertain, experimental portion, or claiming routine development. **Be disciplined; under-supported claims create penalty exposure.** `[manual]` to judge; `[auto]` for the spend-profile evidence in Step 3. **And note the specific hazard of a connected ledger: totalling a whole project is one click, and it is the wrong answer.**

**Software development**: qualifies only where there is genuine technological uncertainty — novel algorithms, performance challenges at the edge of what is known, genuinely uncertain integration — **not routine coding or configuration. Scrutinise carefully.** `[manual]`, and worth flagging as the highest-risk category in most modern claims, because the cost is large, the coding is easy, and the uncertainty frequently is not there. A ledger showing heavy engineering spend says nothing whatsoever about whether it qualifies.

**Grants and subsidies**: a grant may reduce or disqualify relief, or route the project to a different scheme. **Identify all funding.** `[auto]` to find — see Step 4, where this is one of the strongest automatic checks in the skill — `[manual]` to apply.

**Subcontracted / contractor R&D**: the rules on **who** may claim — contractor or principal — and at what percentage vary and are intricate. **Confirm.** `[auto]` for the spend, `[manual]` for the entitlement.

**Staff time apportionment**: needs a defensible basis. **Pure guesses are weak**; time records or structured estimates tied to project involvement are stronger. `[manual]`. **The ledger holds no time.**

**Capital versus revenue**: some regimes have separate R&D capital allowances for assets; the expenditure credit covers revenue costs. **Do not conflate.** `[auto]` and useful: **whether development costs were capitalised or expensed is directly readable.** Capitalised development on the balance sheet is a population that an expenditure-based claim may treat quite differently, and it is easy to miss because it is not in the P&L. See `intangibles-and-amortization`.

**Loss-making companies**: many regimes let loss-makers surrender losses for a cash credit — valuable for startups. **Confirm eligibility and rate.** `[auto]` for the loss position and its size; `[manual]` for eligibility.

**Group / connected company rules**: subcontracting within a group, and which entity claims, follow specific rules. `[auto]` where both entities are connected — read both sides. `[manual]` for entitlement. See Gate 1.

**Claim notification and deadlines**: some jurisdictions require advance notification of an intent to claim, or impose strict deadlines. **Flag.** `[manual]` — and flag it **early in the engagement, not in the output**, because a missed notification deadline can extinguish an otherwise valid claim entirely and no amount of good work afterwards recovers it.

**Changing rules**: R&D regimes are frequently reformed — rates, scope, new compliance requirements. **Always confirm current rules — never hardcode.** `[manual]`. **This is the original's own statement of the seed principle**, and it governs this file too: nothing here supplies a rate, and nothing persisted supplies one either.

**Audit risk**: R&D claims are commonly examined. **Robust technical narratives and cost support are the best defence. Recommend specialist review for material claims.** `[manual]`. The cost support is where the workspace helps; the narratives are where the claim is decided; and **specialist review is not optional on a material claim** merely because the arithmetic was automated.

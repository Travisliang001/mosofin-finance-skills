# Due Diligence Quality of Earnings (QofE) — Mosofin

Performs financial due diligence on a target — analyzing the **quality of reported earnings**, building
**adjusted / normalized EBITDA**, computing the **working capital target** and **net debt** for the deal,
and surfacing risks and observations. Used **buy-side** (the buyer's DD), **sell-side** (vendor due
diligence to support the sale), or for refinancing and exit prep.

**In plain words:** a business is usually priced as a multiple of its profits, so the single most valuable
question in a deal is *which profits are real and repeatable?* A QofE strips out the one-offs, the owner's
personal costs, and the accounting choices that flatter the picture, and asks what a new owner could
actually expect to earn. It also settles two numbers that move the price directly: how much working
capital comes with the business, and what counts as debt.

This skill is **transaction-focused**. It looks at historical financial information with a critical lens
to determine "what's really there" — **which differs from a statutory audit's accuracy focus.** The output
feeds valuation (`business-valuation`) and deal negotiation. Real QofE work involves substantial financial
DD specialist effort; **this skill structures the analysis rigorously.**

It is **workspace-scoped**, and in this skill that has an unusually sharp consequence — see below.

## The decisive question: is the target the connected entity?

**Ask this before anything else.** It determines whether this skill has data at all.

**Sell-side, vendor due diligence, exit prep, or refinancing your own business** — **the target *is* the
connected entity.** In that case a great deal is `[auto]`, including several things that are normally
weeks of data extraction:

- **Multi-year financials, trial balances and the general ledger** — the base and the depth
- **Revenue by customer, by month** — so Step 4's concentration and churn analysis is a read
- **Monthly working capital over 24–36 months** — Step 5's hardest input, normally rebuilt by hand from
  archived reports
- **The debt-like item sweep** — scattered obligations that belong in deal net debt
- **Capex history** — from the fixed-asset accounts, split by what was bought
- **Related-party and owner spend**, where those parties are named as vendors
- **And the single most valuable test in a QofE**: whether the "one-time" items in the bridge **appear
  every year** — see Step 3b

**Buy-side, where the target is someone else's business** — **almost nothing is `[auto]`.** Your workspace
holds your books, not theirs. The target's data arrives as a data-room export, and this skill structures
the analysis of it. **Say so plainly**; the risk otherwise is analysing the acquirer's own figures by
accident, which would be both useless and confusing.

A middle case worth naming: **post-close, or in an arranged data-room connection, the target's file may
itself be connected.** Check at Gate 1 rather than assuming.

**Mosofin is read-only.** Nothing here changes any records; the QofE is analysis.

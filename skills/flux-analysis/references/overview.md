# Flux analysis (Mosofin)

A **flux** (fluctuation) analysis compares each account with a prior period and explains every
movement large enough to matter. It is a standard close and review step: controllers use it to
catch errors before the numbers go out, and auditors use it as an analytical procedure.

**In plain words:** look at what changed since last month and since the same month last year,
pick out the changes big enough to matter, and for each one say why it happened, with evidence.

**Why two thresholds.** A percentage alone flags a 400% rise in a 50 account. An amount alone
misses a cost that doubled while staying small. Requiring both keeps the list short and the
items material. A few things are flagged whatever the size: an account that stops, an account
that starts, a sign that flips, and gross margin moving.

**What the books can explain.** With ledger detail, most movements trace to postings: a larger
invoice, a new contract, a journal posted late, an entry missing. The skill reads those and
names the driver.

**What the books cannot explain.** A price change agreed by phone, a campaign approved in a
meeting, a customer lost: the books show the effect, not the cause. The skill asks the account
owner rather than guessing. An explanation without evidence is marked "unexplained: asked".

**Mosofin is read-only.** It cannot post an entry or send a question. Every entry and every
owner question below is a *proposal* for a person.

**Where it sits.** Comparison with a budget is a different question (budget-vs-actual-analysis).
Reconciling an account to its support is another (balance-sheet-reconciliations). A flux tells
you where to look; those skills check what you find.
